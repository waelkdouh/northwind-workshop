### 1.1 How to read this document

- **Plain words (ES-01).** Acronyms are explained the first time they appear and again in the glossary (section 17).
- **Source cites (ES-02).** Every requirement carries its source, for example `(source: BR-05, CS-10)`. Source ids are quoted exactly as in the knowledge documents and never renumbered.
- **ASSUMPTION: (ES-03).** Anything not stated in `knowledge-docs` is marked **ASSUMPTION:** and listed in section 14 with an owner who can confirm it. An assumption is never presented as a requirement.
- **OPEN: (ES-04).** Every unanswered question is marked **OPEN:** and listed in section 15 with the person who can answer it.
- **Id families.** `BR-` is a business requirement, `NFR-` a non-functional requirement (a quality the solution must have), `C-` a constraint, `SM-` a success measure, `P-` a problem, `CS-` a current-state fact, `ES-` an engineering standard, `BA-` a business area, `OQ-` an open question from the business case and `G-` a gap from the intake review. `BG-` is a business goal, `A-` an assumption, `O-` an open item, `X-` a cross-track dependency and `T-` a tension between sources. `FR-nnn` is a product functional requirement (this document). `EPIC-`, `FEAT-` and `STORY-` are backlog items; `EA-`, `EN-` and `EP-` are enabler stories for the Azure, .NET and Power Platform tracks.
- **Tags.** Every item carries exactly one track tag: `track-azure`, `track-dotnet` or `track-power-platform` (ES-30). An item planned in a release also carries that release tag, for example `azure-r1` (ES-31). Items not yet planned carry only the track tag.

## 2. Summary

Northwind Traders is a business-to-business (B2B) food distributor in Lyon with a second warehouse in Antwerp. It serves about 1,150 active business customers in France, Belgium and Luxembourg. About 1,900 orders a week are keyed in by hand into **Order Desk**, an internal web application built in 2013. Customers cannot see their orders, and 85 delivery claims a week are handled in a shared spreadsheet (source: P-01 to P-06, CS-01, CS-05).

This PRD turns the business requirements of [business-requirements.md](business-requirements.md) into **product requirements** that two delivery teams can build from. It does not repeat the business case.

- **Three business areas, three tracks.** BA-01 (Azure track) moves Order Desk to Microsoft Azure before the data-centre lease ends on 30 June 2027. BA-02 (.NET track) modernises Order Desk and builds the Customer Portal. BA-03 (Power Platform track) replaces the claims spreadsheet. Each track builds only from its own releases, and a track never waits for another (source: business-case section 4, C-01, ES-31).
- **Release plan.** Azure has **3 releases** (`azure-r1` to `azure-r3`) and .NET has **3 releases** (`dotnet-r1` to `dotnet-r3`). Power Platform has **no release and no team** (section 6). The release counts and windows are proposed and are targets, not commitments (A-01, A-41, A-42; **OPEN:** O-25).
- **Requirements.** 57 product functional requirements (`FR-101` to `FR-120` Azure, `FR-201` to `FR-240` .NET, `FR-301` to `FR-330` Power Platform), each tied to a `BR-` or `NFR-`, a track, and a release or "unplanned" (section 7). All 19 `BR-` and 9 `NFR-` are covered and none is left out (section 16).
- **Enablers.** 36 enabler stories do the technical work that the features need first: 16 Azure, 14 .NET and 6 Power Platform (section 9). Two of the Azure enablers, `EA-15` and `EA-16`, give `BR-13` design items for hosting and the database move. They produce checked design, code and migration steps, and nothing is applied in Azure (ES-27; **ASSUMPTION:** A-59).
- **BA-03 is in scope but unplanned.** Its requirements are documented and tagged `track-power-platform` only. The Q1 2027 claims pilot of the business case **cannot be committed** (A-25). **OPEN:** O-07 asks Lena Varga and Marc Dubois for a team, skills and a date.
- **Dependencies between tracks are OPEN items, not waits.** Twenty-three cross-track needs are listed in section 10. Each track can keep delivering without the others.
- **Experiments.** Three experiments test the riskiest assumption of each business area before the epics they gate are committed. Their results are **pending** (section 12).
- **Approval to build is not given.** The business case says "Approved for discovery. Not yet approved for build". **OPEN:** O-21, owner Marc Dubois.

Primary success measure: the share of orders placed through the portal reaches 35 % six months after launch, up from 0 % (source: SM-01). The other measures are in section 4.

## 3. Product vision and users

### 3.1 Product vision

Northwind wants three things (source: business-case section 3):

1. A secure, web-based self-service **Customer Portal** where business customers see and place their own orders (BA-02, BG-01).
2. **Order Desk running in Microsoft Azure** before the data-centre lease ends (BA-01, BG-02).
3. A **delivery-claims** process that replaces the spreadsheet and the e-mail approvals (BA-03, BG-03).

All three stay inside two run-cost caps and keep customer data protected and auditable (BG-04, BG-05).

### 3.2 Problems this product addresses

The problems are quoted in the BRD (section 3.2): `P-01` manual re-keying of about 1,900 orders a week, `P-02` 4.1 % of order lines needing correction, `P-03` about 410 "where is my order?" calls a week, `P-04` 70 % of orders repeating a previous order, `P-05` about 120 invoice copy requests a week and `P-06` about 85 delivery claims a week taking 9 working days with 11 % lost or recorded twice. The mapping of `P-` ids to business areas is the author's inference (**ASSUMPTION:** A-07). BA-01 has no `P-` problem of its own; the lease end and the out-of-support servers drive it (source: C-01, C-02).

## 7. Functional requirements

Each product functional requirement (FR) is tied to a business requirement or a quality requirement, to one track and to one release or "unplanned". The acceptance outcome is in the requirement text; the Given / When / Then criteria are written in the backlog (ES-05).

How priority is set: a requirement that serves a `BR-` takes that `BR-` priority from the business case. A requirement that serves an `NFR-` is Must (**ASSUMPTION:** A-06). An enabler-driven design item takes the priority rule in A-49.

The "Goal" column names the business goal (section 4.1). "Links" lists the enablers, dependencies, assumptions and open items that affect the requirement. A "Gate" is the experiment decision taken before the requirement's epic is committed (section 6.5).
