## 2. Executive summary

Northwind Traders is a business-to-business (B2B) food distributor in Lyon with a second warehouse in Antwerp. It serves about 1,150 active business customers in France, Belgium and Luxembourg. Customers order by phone, e-mail or a PDF form, and 38 Order Desk staff re-key every order into **Order Desk**, an internal web application built in 2013 that runs in the Lyon data centre (source: business-case section 1 and 2, CS-01, CS-05).

The business wants three things (source: business-case section 3):

1. A secure self-service **Customer Portal** where business customers see and place their own orders (business area BA-02, **.NET** track).
2. **Order Desk running in Microsoft Azure** before the data-centre lease ends on 30 June 2027 (BA-01, **Azure** track) (source: C-01).
3. A **delivery-claims** process that replaces the shared spreadsheet and the e-mail approvals (BA-03, **Power Platform** track).

The work is split into three independent business areas. Each area has its own delivery track and builds only from its own numbered releases. A track never waits for another track; a dependency is written down as an open item (source: business-case section 4, ES-31).

- The Azure track plans **3 releases** and the .NET track plans **3 releases** (section 10).
- The Power Platform track has **no release and no team** now. Its business areas are fully documented and in scope, but the **Q1 2027 claims pilot cannot be committed** (A-25). **OPEN:** O-07 asks Lena Varga and Marc Dubois for a team, skills and a date.

Primary success measure: the share of orders placed through the portal reaches 35 % six months after launch, up from 0 % (source: SM-01). Other measures are in section 4.

The business case is "approved for discovery, not yet approved for build". The release dates in this BRD are targets only (A-01, A-42). **OPEN:** O-21 asks Marc Dubois for the approval and funding to build.

## 3. Context and problems

### 3.1 Who Northwind is

Northwind Traders is a B2B distributor of specialty food products based in Lyon, with a second warehouse in Antwerp. It supplies about 1,150 active business customers: independent grocers, delicatessens, restaurants and hotel kitchens. It employs 240 people, 38 of them in Customer Operations (the "Order Desk") (source: business-case section 1, CS-03, CS-05).

### 3.2 Problems today

The problem statements are quoted from the business case. The "Business area" column is the author's inference (**ASSUMPTION:** A-07, owner Lena Varga): the business case lists no `P-` ids against business areas.

| Id | Problem (quoted figures) | Business area (inferred, A-07) | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-01 | About **1,900 orders per week** are entered manually. 62 % arrive by phone or e-mail. | BA-02 | BG-01 |
| P-02 | Re-keying causes errors: **4.1 %** of order lines need a correction after entry (wrong product, wrong quantity, wrong delivery address). | BA-02 | BG-01 |
| P-03 | Customers cannot see the status of their orders. The Order Desk receives about **410 "where is my order?" calls per week**, roughly 35 % of all inbound calls. | BA-02 | BG-01 |
| P-04 | 70 % of orders repeat a previous order with small changes, but customers must dictate or re-type the full list every time. | BA-02 | BG-01 |
| P-05 | Customers ask for copies of invoices by e-mail. Staff search a network file share for the PDF and send it by hand (about 120 requests per week). | BA-02 | BG-01 |
| P-06 | About **85 delivery claims per week** (damaged, missing or wrong products) arrive by phone or e-mail. Staff record them in a shared spreadsheet and Finance approves credit notes by e-mail. A claim takes **9 working days** on average to resolve, and **11 %** of claims are lost or recorded twice. | BA-03 | BG-03 |

BA-01 has no `P-` problem of its own. The closest drivers are the lease end and the out-of-support servers (source: C-01, C-02, A-07).

### 3.3 Current state that shapes the work

These facts come from `current-state.md`. They are context, not requirements.

- Order Desk is a web application (ASP.NET MVC 5, .NET Framework 4.8) built in 2013 and kept up by the internal IT team. About 60 users work in it at the same time at peak (Monday 08:00 to 11:00) (source: CS-01, CS-03).
- Staff sign in with Windows Integrated Authentication against the company's own Active Directory. No customer can sign in today (source: CS-04).
- The web server runs Windows Server 2012 R2 and the database runs SQL Server 2014 (48 GB, growing about 1 GB a month). Both are past end of support (source: CS-06, CS-07, C-02).
- The warehouse system **StockPilot** (Antwerp, on premises) exchanges files with Order Desk in a nightly batch at 02:00. Order statuses Picking and Shipped therefore change once per night (source: CS-10, C-03).
- Invoices are PDF files written by the finance system to a network share (about 120 GB) and read by Order Desk (source: CS-08, CS-11).
- There is no automated build or deployment, and the last disaster-recovery test was never done (source: CS-19, CS-23).
- Order Desk has no application programming interface (API). Other systems exchange files with it or read its database directly (source: CS-27).
- Claims live in a spreadsheet of about 9,400 rows edited by up to 10 people at once. The finance system has no API; it can import a CSV file (a plain-text table) placed in a share before 18:00 on working days (source: CS-24, CS-25).
- Power Platform today has only the default environment, with 23 personal apps and flows and no data loss prevention (DLP) policy (source: CS-26).

## 10. Delivery tracks and release strategy

### 10.1 Rules

- Each business area is delivered by its own team, on its own track, from its own numbered releases (source: business-case section 4, ES-30, ES-31).
- A release never waits for another track. A need from another track is written down as an **OPEN:** item in the release file of the track that needs it (ES-31, A-45).
- A release is numbered `<track>-r<n>` (for example `azure-r1`). The planned Git tag is `product/<track>-r<n>`. **No tag exists yet.** Tags are created only after the product owner approves a release (ES-31, A-39).
